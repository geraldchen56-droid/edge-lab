Kelly Sizing

Used in: Higher or Lower, Betting Board, poker bankroll, position sizing

Positive EV is not enough: bet too much and a losing run wipes you out. Kelly picks the fraction of bankroll that maximises long-run growth. With win probability p and net odds b (win b per 1 staked):

f* = (b·p − (1 − p)) / b = edge / odds even money: f* = 2p − 1

Betting twice Kelly gives roughly zero long-run growth, and more than that shrinks the bankroll even though every bet is +EV. Most professionals bet half Kelly, because their p is only an estimate.

Under the hood: reading f* = (b·p − q) ÷ b
f*The fraction of your current bankroll to stake. The star marks it as the optimal value.
pProbability you win the bet.
q = 1 − pProbability you lose.
bNet odds: profit per 1 staked if you win. Even money is b = 1. From decimal odds d, b = d − 1.
b·p − qEV per 1 staked: you win b with probability p and lose 1 with probability q. This is your edge.
÷ bScale down for long-shot payoffs, which swing more. So the result is edge divided by odds.

Why not just maximise EV?

Maximising EV says stake everything on every +EV bet, and the first loss takes you to zero. Wealth compounds by multiplying, not adding: +50% followed by −50% leaves you at 1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75 of where you started. So Kelly maximises the average growth rate, which is the expected logarithm of the multiplier on each bet:

g(f) = p · ln(1 + f·b) + q · ln(1 − f)

Here ln(1 + f·b) is the log of your wealth multiplier on a win, and ln(1 − f) is the log of the multiplier on a loss. Set the slope of g to zero, p·b ÷ (1 + f·b) = q ÷ (1 − f), and solving for f gives f* = (b·p − q) ÷ b.

Example: p = 0.55, b = 1 → f* = 0.10. Growth per bet = 0.55 · ln 1.1 + 0.45 · ln 0.9 = 0.0524 − 0.0474 = 0.0050, about 0.5% per bet, compounding.

Shorthand
  • Even money: bet your edge. 55% win → 10% of bankroll.
  • Negative EV → bet 0. Kelly never says bet on a losing proposition.
  • Half Kelly keeps about 75% of the growth with far smaller drawdowns.
