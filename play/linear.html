Linearity & Spread

Used in: Dice, Trading Game, Card Game

The expected value of a sum equals the sum of the expected values. This holds even when the parts are dependent, which is why you never list 36 outcomes to price two dice.

E[X + Y] = E[X] + E[Y]

Variances add only when the parts are independent. So σ grows with the square root of the number of parts: ten dice have ten times the EV of one, but only about 3.2 times the spread. This is why sums look like a bell curve and why your quote width should grow slower than your fair value.

σ(sum of n independent) = σ₁ · √n
Under the hood: why EVs always add but spread doesn't

Reading E[X + Y] = E[X] + E[Y]

E[ · ]"The expected value of" whatever is inside the brackets.
X, YTwo random quantities, such as die 1 and die 2.
X + YA new random quantity: their total on each roll.

Why it always holds. An average is "add everything, then divide", and the order you add things in doesn't matter. Over 600 rolls of two dice, the grand total of all the sums equals the total of die 1's column plus the total of die 2's column. Dependence changes which values appear together on the same row, but not what each column adds up to.

Reading Var(X + Y) = Var(X) + Var(Y) + 2·Cov(X, Y)

Cov(X, Y)Covariance: do X and Y tend to be high together (positive), move in opposite directions (negative), or ignore each other (zero)? It is the average of (X − μₓ)(Y − μᵧ).
independentKnowing one tells you nothing about the other. Then Cov = 0 and the variances simply add.

The last row is a hedge: two risky positions whose errors cancel exactly. All three setups have EV 7, so only the spread differs.

Where √n comes from

Add n independent copies, each with variance σ₁². The variances add to n·σ₁², so σ = √(n·σ₁²) = σ₁·√n. Ten dice: 1.71 × √10 ≈ 5.40, against an EV of 35. In finance, this is why diversification works: holding many assets that aren't perfectly correlated grows expected return faster than it grows risk.

Shorthand
  • Sum → add EVs. n dice = 3.5n. n cards without replacement = 7n.
  • Spread → √n. n dice σ ≈ 1.71√n. Roughly 95% of outcomes fall within 2σ.
  • Known part → plug it in. One die shows 5: fair for two dice is 5 + 3.5 = 8.5.
  • Without replacement: unknown cards average the remaining deck, not 7.
